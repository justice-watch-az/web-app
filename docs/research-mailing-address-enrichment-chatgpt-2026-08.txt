# AZ DUI Lead — Mailing Address Enrichment

**A. Data Sources & Vendors:** For name(+DOB/ZIP)→address lookup, the market has both low-cost pay-per-hit services and higher-end investigative systems.  **Budget APIs (pay-per-match):** Examples include **SkipReach**, **Tracerfy**, **Apify’s Skip Trace**, **Searchbug**, and **DataSkip**. These let any business sign up online with minimal credentialing, charge per successful match (no subscription), and return addresses/phones/emails from public records.  For instance, SkipReach offers a REST API ($0.05 per hit, no monthly fee) returning addresses, phones, emails, etc. on a “pay only for hits” basis.  Tracerfy is a real-estate–focused API ($0.02 per record) that returns current+prior mailing addresses, phone (with type), email, age, and DNC flags.  Apify’s “Skip Trace” actor (community-run) charges **$6.50 per 1,000 matches** (≈$0.0065 each) and returns a merged person record with aliases, current and previous addresses, normalized phones, verified emails, relatives, and a match-confidence score.  Searchbug (a people-search API) has tiered pricing ~$0.77→$0.30 per match and returns address history, phones, emails, etc.  DataSkip’s address-to-owner API charges 4¢ per match (misses free), taking a property address and returning owner name, mailing address (if different), phones (with DNC flags), and emails.  **DataZapp** offers phone/email append for property-owner lists (3¢/match with volume discounts).  These budget services are typically public-data–only (not FCRA agencies) and usable by any business.  

**Mid-Tier / Investigative Databases:** These require credentials or subscriptions.  **Tracers** (“investigative software”) offers web and API access to ~42B records.  Users must register as an investigator and buy credits or a plan. It provides address history, phone numbers, emails, relatives, assets, etc., via both real-time and batch queries (first $100 of searches free) and requires annual contracts at hundreds-of-dollars/month (volume-discounted).  **LocatePLUS** provides unlimited person searches for a flat monthly fee (plans from ~$39.95/mo) and returns addresses, phones, vehicles, property, criminal records, etc. via a web portal or limited API; it requires business verification (typically investigators or legal pros) and credentials.  **IDI / IRB Search** (Investigative Data Info) is tailored for collections: real-time credit-header lookups (name-to-address/phone), batch skip tracing, employment and asset data.  It’s sold by subscription to debt-collectors and attorneys (FCRA-permissible use only).  **Delvepoint** is a newer cloud skip-trace platform that touts no long-term contracts, extensive public/private records, saved searches and relationship graphs.  Users say it has no setup fees and credit-based pricing (likely similar to Tracers).  **Enterprise tools:**  **LexisNexis Accurint/CLEAR** and **TLOxp** (TransUnion) are the legal-industry standards. These require costly annual contracts, KYC, and FCRA licensure.  They offer comprehensive public records and credit-header data (historical addresses, court filings, etc.) with advanced link analysis.  Expect *no case-number search*: no vendor lets you input a court case to retrieve addresses.  

**Auth & Usage:**  Cheap APIs (SkipReach, Apify, DataSkip, Searchbug) use standard API keys or web credentials; any business can buy access online.  They support REST calls and often bulk/CSV uploads.  Investigative services (Tracers, LocatePLUS, IRB) require vetting (proof of PI license, EIN, or professional role) and come with logged access.  Tracers/LocatePLUS offer batch processing and webforms; Tracerfy and DataSkip offer synchronous or async bulk CSV jobs.  Rate limits vary: cheap APIs typically allow hundreds of requests/minute, while enterprise tools have higher thresholds.  **Cost per lookup:** ranges from < $0.01 (Apify at $0.0065/match; Tracerfy $0.02) to a few cents (DataSkip $0.04, DataZapp ~$0.03) to ~$0.05 (SkipReach) or $0.10+.  Tracers/LocatePLUS expect ~$0.10-$0.50 per record (credit-based).  TLO/Accurint are much higher (hundreds to thousands monthly).  Vendors boast hit rates: budget services often have ~70–90% return rate, while Tracers/Accurint cite ~98% coverage of U.S. adults.  Freshness: APIs claim real-time updated data.  **Data fields:**  Most return current/residential addresses (and past addresses), phone numbers (landline/mobile), emails, relatives, and confidence scores. For example, Apify’s output includes `current_address`, `previous_addresses`, `phones`, `emails`, `relatives`, and a `matchConfidence` flag.  Tracerfy returns current+prior mailing addresses, phones with carrier info, and deceased/Litigator flags.  LocatePLUS and Tracers also return assets and public records.  

**Free AZ Data (for support only):**  Arizona’s voter registration list (available to political campaigns and public inspection) includes name, residence/mailing addresses, ZIP, birth year, phone, etc..  **However, use is restricted** to election/political purposes, so it can’t be used for commercial attorney marketing.  County property/assessor databases (e.g. Maricopa Recorder search) are public: you can manually look up a name to find real property addresses, assessed value, etc.  These can *support* skip-trace results but are not easily automated in bulk.  Motor Vehicle (MVD) records are off-limits to non-permissible users (DPPA).  Court documents sometimes list a defendant’s last-known city (but rarely full address).  In sum, **official public signals** (voter, property) can help verify an address but cannot legally drive a direct mail campaign. Consumer lookup sites (WhitePages/BeenVerified, etc.) use public/social data but lack permissible-purpose restrictions; they are low-cost ($20–40/mo) and sometimes useful as a secondary check.

**B. Matching & Ranking:**  To maximize match quality, provide as many unique identifiers as possible.  **Key inputs:** full name, date of birth (or birth year), middle name/initial, and last-known location (city/ZIP).  Adding a middle name or DOB dramatically reduces false matches.  If case # is given, it’s not directly usable by data vendors.  Many skip-trace systems accept partial address or age range fields to refine.  **Disambiguation:** When multiple candidates share a name, use all available clues: DOB, middle initial, suffix, and recent activity.  Cross-check relative names (siblings/spouse) or employer if known.  Tier-1 databases employ alias/nickname logic: for example, LexisNexis Accurint automatically searches common nicknames/maiden names and verifies addresses.  Vendors like Apify compute a `matchConfidence` score and flag the single “mostLikely” record.  In practice, you might feed a name and city to multiple vendors and look for overlapping addresses.  

**Ranking candidates:**  Once you have a list of possible addresses for a person, rank by: 
- **Proximity to court:** Geocode each address (e.g. via Google/Mapbox) and compute distance to the defendant’s arraignment location.  Closer addresses are more likely current residences.  
- **Address recency:** Prefer addresses where the “move-in” date is most recent.  Some APIs (e.g. Tracerfy) sort by recency of records. 
- **Residential vs. non-residential:** Flag PO boxes or businesses.  Skip those in favor of home addresses. 
- **Vendor confidence:** Use provided confidence flags.  If an API marks one address as `mostLikely` or gives higher matchConfidence, treat it higher.  
For example, Apify’s result sets include a boolean `mostLikely` on one address, and Tracerfy ranks phones and addresses by recency and source consensus.  Combining these criteria (distance *and* confidence) will surface the strongest candidate.  

**Success rates:**  In practice, a high percentage of leads will yield *some* address.  Industry reports show skip-trace hit rates often between 65–92%.  That suggests roughly 70–90% of leads will produce at least one valid mailing address or phone.  However, many subjects have multiple past addresses.  Expect that in perhaps **40–60%** of cases one address clearly stands out (recent, residential, highest confidence); others may need the secretary’s judgment.  Vendors claim high coverage: Tracers/IDI promise data on over 98% of U.S. adults, but real-world effective hit rates (for updated current address) will be lower.  Conservative planning: ~80% of leads yield ≥1 usable address; a clear “single best” address might occur ~50% of the time.  

**C. Product & Architecture:**  
- **On-Demand vs Batch:** Many systems support both real-time API calls and bulk batch jobs.  On-demand lookup (when the lead enters the system) gives immediate results but is pricier per record.  Batch mode (e.g. nightly CSV job) can exploit any volume pricing and reduce manual work.  For example, SkipReach and DataSkip let you upload thousands of records in CSV; their dashboards quote costs before charging.  Tracers and Delvepoint offer batch skip trace services.  An on-demand call might be best if you must mail within 24h, whereas a daily batch can cheaply enrich all new leads overnight.  Note compliance: batch processing of skip traces still requires each search to have a permissible purpose on record. Logging (which vendor, time, and query inputs) should be tracked per lead whether batch or on-demand.  

- **Data Model:**  Store each lead with its candidate addresses (and any phone numbers).  For each candidate record, log the **source vendor**, retrieval **timestamp**, and any confidence score or flag.  Keep fields like “address, city, ZIP, type (home/PO), move-in date, distance-to-court, confidence score.”  Once a secretary picks an address, mark it (and timestamp) as “chosen” in the DB.  Maintain the raw vendor responses for audit/feedback.  As a minimal model, you might have a table `LeadSearch` (lead info) and `Candidates` (address, vendor, score, chosen flag).  

- **Geocoding:**  Yes – geocode the court location and all candidate addresses.  This allows computing straight-line or driving distance.  Google Maps or Mapbox APIs can be used (billing per request); open options like Nominatim exist but may have rate limits.  Distance is a key rank factor, so include latitude/longitude in your storage.  

- **MVP Scope:**  Focus first on addresses (physical mailing).  Phones and emails can be collected in parallel (many skip-trace APIs return them), but *do not use them for contacting* until you have clear compliance sign-off (TCPA rules).  For now, mark phone/email as “future feature.”  Logging them is fine, but SMS/call should be out-of-scope MVP.  

**D. Legal & Ethical Considerations (AZ):**  Arizona’s ethics rules allow attorney marketing by mail.  **ER 7.3** prohibits live person-to-person solicitation (“cold calling”), but explicitly *permits* sending letters or postcards unless the recipient has opted out.  So mailed outreach to a defendant is allowed, provided it’s not misleading.  Per ER 7.1, any communication must include the lawyer’s name/firm and honest content.  In practice, ensure your mailer clearly says “Attorney at Law” and is not deceptive.  There is no Arizona bar rule banning mailed solicitations post-arrest (unlike some states’ “legal advertising” bans).  The *tech vendor* should ensure the UI forces any ad copy to include contact info, lawyer name, and disclaimers.  The *law firm* must comply with ARPC 7.1–7.3 in their mail content, but the data service itself is not practicing law.  

**Privacy/TCPA:**  This project avoids calls/SMS entirely, so the TCPA (telephone consumer protection) concerns are minimal.  (If phones are added later, ensure any calls/SMS require prior express consent and DNC scrubbing, etc.)  Focus on postal mail and data: skip-trace addresses are derived from public and licensed data, not inherently regulated by TCPA.  

**FCRA & Data Use:**  Skip tracing often involves “consumer report” data (credit headers, etc.), which normally triggers FCRA if used for covered purposes.  Importantly, **using skip-trace data for attorney solicitation is *not* one of FCRA’s regulated purposes** (like credit or insurance).  Skipreach notes that using tracing for marketing or locating (non-credit uses) “does not trigger FCRA” requirements.  However, many vendors nonetheless require you to attest a legitimate purpose.  For example, 1SkipTrace’s terms forbid use of their data for “curiosity, stalking, harassment” and require you certify a permissible purpose under law.  In practice, skip-trace services aimed at lawyers typically assume the purpose is litigation/process or collections.  Thus, **avoid violating vendor terms**: keep records (e.g. court case number) to justify why you looked up that person.  Many vendors (Tracers, Accurint) require PI licensure or an attorney letter of representation to use their FCRA-level data.  Others (SkipReach, DataSkip, Apify, Delvepoint) are “non-CRA” and primarily public-data; these permit broader use but still forbid unlawful ends.  

**Summary:** Use a combination of tools. Start MVP with a pay-per-look API (e.g. SkipReach or Apify) for real-time enrichment and supplement with a trier service (e.g. Tracerfy or DataSkip). Batch-run candidates if volume is high. Present all address candidates to the secretary sorted by distance to court and confidence. Record chosen results for analytics. Finally, ensure all marketing material complies with Arizona ethics (lawyer ID in every mailer) and respect opt-out requests. Keep abreast of vendor data-use terms and FCRA: treat the lookup as skip-tracing for litigation, not credit decisions.
